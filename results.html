TrackMaze — when does a network memorize vs track?

A controllable egocentric maze for state-tracking under OOD. Results below are recomputed by bench/analysis_A.py — no hardcoded numbers.

Does a model just memorize a reactive “see X → do Y” rule, or does it genuinely track and correct its latent position from the first-person stream — and how does the underlying spatial drift scale with path length, maze size, cue ambiguity, and data diversity?

A · drift law
The latent position estimate drifts, and it drifts lawfully — error grows smoothly over the episode and is worse on bigger mazes. That clean, path-length-scaling drift is itself the evidence that a real recurrent state computation emerged (a lookup table wouldn’t drift on a curve).
B · memorization
Giving the net the landmark cues makes it worse on unseen mazes than using no cues at all. It overfits each training maze’s landmark layout (a non-transferable shortcut) instead of learning to correct its latent — so cues that an oracle uses to halve error are a trap for standard nets.
C · diversity
That memorization shortcut is suppressed by data diversity: as the number of distinct training worlds grows past the net’s capacity to memorize each, the harm from landmarks shrinks toward zero — a measurable memorize→generalize transition driven by diversity-vs-capacity.
D · the threshold
Even with a perfect re-anchor, correction only works while cues are distinguishable. As landmark ambiguity rises, data-association fails (you match the wrong look-alike place) and the correction degrades to useless — a graceful threshold, not a cliff.
In one line. Real state-tracking (path integration) does emerge and drifts lawfully; standard nets memorize visual cues rather than using them to correct that drift; the memorization is a diversity-vs-capacity effect; and correct cue-use is bounded by data-association (ambiguity) — together a quantified account of the drift law and the memorize↔track boundary.  ·  Play the interactive maze: demo.
